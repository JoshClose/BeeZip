# BeeZip — M10 (NSIS and CramFS) Plan

This plan builds on [`PLAN.md`](PLAN.md) and [`M0.md`](M0.md)–[`M9.md`](M9.md) and keeps their
decisions. After 0.4.0 the user asked whether the last two formats only 7-Zip reads, NSIS
installers and CramFS images, could be read without it (2026-11-12). After M9 they are the only
formats left to 7-Zip besides MSI patches, transforms and merge modules (M9 §6).

A spike on this machine backs the plan (`target/m10-spikes/`, with a `REPORT.md`; §14 sums it
up). A prototype of both readers was checked against 7-Zip 26.00 file by file, fuzzed, and
timed against 7-Zip and BeeZip 0.4.0 (which reads both through 7-Zip). It is about 1,600 lines
of Rust, built only on crates BeeZip already has (flate2/zlib-rs, lzma-rust2, crc32fast), with
an inflater and an NSIS bzip2 decoder of its own. Its samples:
- **CramFS:** 13 images from mkfs.cramfs 2.39.3, in both byte orders, with 4 to 64 KiB blocks,
  holes, a 512-byte boot pad, and a 112 MB tree of `/usr`. Two crafted ones: Linux 4.15's
  extended block pointers, and 7-Zip's LZMA variant. And the fixture.
- **NSIS:** 57 installers:
  - 14 found on this machine: Steam's and VLC's uninstallers, Signal's and Another Redis Desktop
    Manager's installers and uninstallers, BlueStacks', Razer's, MuMu Player's, clink's, Tauri's
    own NSIS, and two built by NSIS forks;
  - BeeZip's own 0.1.x installers, M6's samples and the three fixtures;
  - 23 made with makensis 3.09 and 2.50 (Unicode, ANSI and 64-bit; zlib, bzip2 and LZMA; solid
    and not) and 5 large ones from the same 112 MB tree;
  - four official NSIS 2 installers downloaded for the spike: NSIS 2.46's and 2.51's own, 7-Zip
    9.20's and VLC 2.2.8's.

These findings shape the plan:
- **CramFS: every file 7-Zip extracts without an error is identical to the prototype's** (5,575
  files). The prototype also reads what 7-Zip can't:
  - files with holes (7-Zip: "Data Error");
  - images of 8 to 64 KiB blocks (7-Zip: "Unsupported Method" on every file over 4 KiB);
  - images padded for a boot block (7-Zip can't open them, though BeeZip's detection already
    claims them);
  - Linux 4.15's extended block pointers (7-Zip reads that flag as a block size).
- **NSIS: every file 7-Zip extracts is identical, under the same name** (24,308 of the 24,971
  files the prototype reads in 55 installers). The rest are 617 files 7-Zip fails on, 11 it
  leaves out, and 35 whose names it spells another way (§14).
- **NSIS's deflate isn't quite deflate up to NSIS 2.51.** NSIS 2 wrote a stored block's length
  without its complement, and its installers read only the length. NSIS 3 switched to zlib's
  deflate in 2010 (NSIS bug #2995455). zlib-rs rejects NSIS 2's form; BlueStacks' uninstaller
  has five such blocks. So BeeZip needs an inflater of its own, choosing the form by NSIS
  version as 7-Zip does (§3.2).
- **NSIS's bzip2 isn't bzip2:** no stream header, a one-byte block mark, no CRCs and no
  randomised bit. The bzip2 crate can't read it; the prototype's decoder is 270 lines.
- **Exact sizes cost a decode when the installer opens.** 7-Zip lists no size for 3,705 of
  14,396 files (compressed files of non-solid installers, the last file of a solid one, and
  uninstallers) and a wrong one for `StartMenu.dll` in all four of BeeZip's own installers.
  Decoding gives every size, at about 7-Zip's `t` speed (§14): 0.4 s for BeeZip's 11 MB
  installer, against 0.03 s for 7-Zip's listing.
- **Extraction gets much faster.** BeeZip 0.4.0 extracts NSIS installers through a staging folder,
  because 7-Zip's sizes can't be trusted (M6 §3.4): 8.6 s for a 21 MB installer that `7z x`
  extracts in 1.4 s, and 1.7 s against 0.3 s for BeeZip's own. BeeZip's reader streams, so the
  staging folder goes.
- **The uninstaller:** the prototype rebuilds it as NSIS's `WriteUninstaller` does (the
  installer's stub with its icon patched, then the uninstaller's data), and 7-Zip opens the
  result. 7-Zip leaves it out of BeeZip's own installers: its guess at the instruction layout
  marks one of Tauri's instructions bad.
- **No crate fits.**
  - `nsis` 0.6.0 (Apache-2.0) holds the whole installer and its solid stream in memory (64 MiB
    by default). It reads deflate with flate2, so it misses NSIS 2's stored blocks, and it
    brings a second lzma-rust2.
  - `newtua-nsis` is LGPL-3.0.
  - For CramFS, fstool 0.5.2 (already built for ext and NTFS) has a reader behind a `cramfs`
    feature that adds `compcol`, a second inflate. `cramfs` 0.1.0 and `lamfold-cramfs` read 4
    KiB blocks only.
  - Auto mode refuses to build downloaded crates here, so they were read, not run.
- **Fuzzing found one prototype bug** (a cut superblock read before its length was checked) and
  nothing else in 1.4 million runs: no hangs, no run over 2 s, and the heap never over 22 MiB
  (§14).
- **mkfs.cramfs bug:** a name of 253 to 255 bytes wraps the 6-bit length field to 0, writing a
  directory nothing can read, with no warning (util-linux 2.39.3 and master; §14).

Nothing was built, committed or pushed while planning. The plan was approved on 2026-11-12
(§13), and PRs `m10-cramfs` and `m10-nsis` carry it (§9).

## Scope

- **CramFS and NSIS read by BeeZip** (§3–§5): list, test, extract, preview and `entry.read`, and
  Info, on every platform. Variants BeeZip doesn't read go to 7-Zip when it's installed (§5.3).
- **Unchanged:** the formats' wire names (`cramfs`, `nsis`), detection, the commands, Explorer's
  menu and the associations. Neither format is in the menu or associated: CramFS shares `.img`
  with the disk images, and an NSIS installer is an `.exe`. `formats.list` reports both with no
  backend.
- **Still through 7-Zip:** MSI patches, transforms and merge modules (`.msp`, `.mst`, `.msm`),
  and the variants of BeeZip's formats it doesn't read (§6).

## 1. Dependencies

No new crates:
- CramFS's zlib pages use flate2 (zlib-rs).
- NSIS's LZMA and its x86 filter use lzma-rust2 0.25.
- crc32fast checks both formats' checksums.
- NSIS's deflate and bzip2 are BeeZip's own (§3.2), in `format/nsis/`.

Rejected, with the reasons in §14 (decisions 1 and 3):
- **`nsis` 0.6.0** (Apache-2.0): whole installer and solid stream in memory, no NSIS 2 stored
  blocks, a second lzma-rust2 (0.21).
- **`newtua-nsis` 0.1.1:** LGPL-3.0.
- **fstool 0.5.2's `cramfs` feature:** adds `compcol` and its own inflate.
- **`cramfs` 0.1.0 and `lamfold-cramfs` 0.1.0:** 4 KiB blocks only; `cramfs` adds binrw and
  four more crates.

## 2. How it fits

- **Routing.** `External::native()` gains `Cramfs` and `Nsis`, and `source.rs`'s `native()` opens
  them with `cramfs::open` and `nsis::open`. As in M7–M9, `UnsupportedOperation` (never
  `Corrupt` or `NotFound`) goes to 7-Zip when it's installed, with a note in Info. Without 7-Zip
  it fails with `BackendUnavailable`, saying what BeeZip couldn't read.
- **Each is its own `ArchiveReader`** (`format/cramfs/`, `format/nsis/`), as CHM and MSI are. Both
  read through `Source`.
- **Detection doesn't change.** CramFS's magic at 0 or 512, and NSIS's first header at the 512-byte
  boundaries of an executable's overlay (M6 §3.2, `detect.rs`'s `nsis_in`). The NSIS reader
  starts at the offset detection found. NSIS forks with other first-header flags (NSISBI's large
  installers) still aren't claimed, and 7-Zip doesn't open them either.
- **`checks_data` becomes true for both.** A version 2 CramFS image and an NSIS installer each
  carry a CRC-32 of themselves, which `archive.test` checks (§4.4); only WIM had one before.
- **NSIS's staging folder stays** for the NSIS installers that go to 7-Zip (§5.3), as M9 kept
  `backend/slt.rs`'s CHM rules. `slt.rs`'s CramFS name mapping (`oem_names`) stays for the same
  reason.
- **Size:** the prototype's readers are about 300 lines for CramFS and 750 for NSIS, plus 310 for
  the inflater and 270 for bzip2. With the `ArchiveReader`s, Info, limits and errors: about 900
  lines for `format/cramfs/` and 2,500 for `format/nsis/`, plus their tests and hand-made
  writers (§8).

## 3. The formats, as the spike found them

### 3.1 CramFS

- **The superblock,** at 0 or after a 512-byte boot block:
  - the magic, in either byte order, which sets the order of every field after it;
  - `Compressed ROMFS`, the flags and the image's size;
  - a CRC, an edition, block and file counts and a 16-byte name;
  - the root's inode.
- **Inodes are 12 bytes:** mode (16 bits), uid (16), size (24), gid (8), the name's length in 4-byte
  units (6) and an offset in 4-byte units (26). A big-endian image packs them from the top. A
  folder is one run of its children's inodes, each followed by its NUL-padded name.
- **Files and symlinks** are a table of block pointers (each block's end), then the blocks, each a
  zlib stream (with its header). A block of length 0 is a hole: zeros.
- **Linux 4.15's extended pointers** (flag 0x800): bit 31 marks a stored block, bit 30 a direct
  pointer (the block's start, shifted by 2; a compressed direct block starts with its 16-bit
  length).
- **The block size isn't stored.** It's the page size of the machine the image is for: 4 KiB on x86
  and most ARM. Decision 2: the reader takes it from the layout. A file of more than one block
  must have its first block start right after its pointer table, with a zlib header, so the
  first such files settle it (fstool does the same). With no such file it's 4 KiB.
- **Devices** keep their number in the size field; FIFOs and sockets have no data.
- **Version 2 images** (all 13) carry a CRC-32 of the image, from the superblock to the stated
  size with the CRC field zeroed. The 12 readable ones check.
- **7-Zip's own variant** puts a block size and a method (LZMA) in flag bits 11 to 15. Linux doesn't
  mount it, and bit 11 is Linux's extended-pointer flag.

### 3.2 NSIS

- **The first header:** flags (uninstaller, silent, CRC options), `EF BE AD DE`, `NullsoftInst`,
  the header's length, and the length of everything after the first header, CRC included.
- **The method isn't stored:** each compiler stub is built for one. It's read from the data, as
  7-Zip does:
  - LZMA's properties (`5D 00 00 …`), with or without a filter byte before them. The filter is
    BCJ x86, which only old 7-Zip installers used; none of the 57 has it.
  - NSIS's bzip2 block mark (`31`, then a byte under 14).
  - Otherwise deflate.
  - An uncompressed header (MuMu's tools) leaves the method to each file's own data.
- **Solid installers** are one stream: the header's length, the header, then each file as its
  length and bytes. **Non-solid ones** have the header first (its length's top bit set when
  compressed), then each file as its own record, compressed or stored (the top bit again).
- **The header** has flags, then eight block descriptors: pages, sections, instructions, strings,
  language tables, colours, fonts and data. A 64-bit installer's descriptors have 8-byte offsets.
  Instructions are 28 bytes: an opcode and six parameters.
- **Strings** are ANSI or UTF-16; a Unicode table starts with two zero bytes. Codes in them mark
  variables, shell folders and language strings: 1 to 4 in NSIS 3, 252 to 255 in NSIS 2's ANSI,
  and 0xE000 to 0xE003 in Jim Park's Unicode fork. Which codes appear gives the version: 37 NSIS
  3 Unicode, 7 NSIS 3 ANSI, 10 NSIS 2 ANSI and 1 Park Unicode in the spike.
- **Deflate:** NSIS up to 2.51 wrote stored blocks with LEN and no NLEN (its own zlib); NSIS 3 is
  standard. The reader reads NSIS 2's form for NSIS 2 and Park installers and the standard one
  for NSIS 3. Where a record doesn't decode in that form, or doesn't end at its record's end,
  it tries the other. That second chance reads makensis builds linked to the system's zlib, such
  as Ubuntu 16.04's 2.50, whose own installers can't read their stored blocks.
- **bzip2:** blocks of up to 900 kB, each starting with the byte `31` in the bit stream, then the
  24-bit origin pointer and the usual block. The stream ends with `17`. There are no CRCs.
- **The CRC** is a CRC-32 of the file from byte 512 to the end of the NSIS data, as NSIS's loader
  checks it, when the flags ask for one. 53 installers check; 1 has none; M6's damaged copy
  fails.
- **Identical data is stored once:** several `File` instructions can point at one record (four
  names share one in the made installers).
- **The uninstaller is two records:** the icon patch for the installer's stub, then the
  uninstaller's own NSIS data. NSIS's `WriteUninstaller` copies the stub, patches it and appends
  the data.

## 4. Reading

### 4.1 The listing

**CramFS:**
- folders, files and symlinks with their targets, as 7-Zip lists them;
- devices, FIFOs and sockets as `Other`, as BeeZip's squashfs reader lists them (7-Zip writes
  them as empty files);
- mode, uid and gid; no times, since CramFS stores none;
- names from their bytes: UTF-8, then the `encoding` option. 7-Zip for Windows reads them in the
  OEM code page, which BeeZip undoes today (`slt.rs`'s `oem_names`).
- Identical files share data but list as files; CramFS has no hard links.

**NSIS** (decision 4), by 7-Zip's rules, so nothing changes for someone who had 7-Zip:
- **Output folders:** a stack of output folders from `SetOutPath` (`CreateDirectory` with its
  output-path parameter set), with `$OUTDIR` and `$_OUTDIR` replaced by the current folder.
- **`File` instructions:** each is listed under the current folder, unless its name starts with
  `$INSTDIR`, `$EXEDIR`, `$TEMP` or `$PLUGINSDIR`, a drive or `\\`.
- **Names:** `$INSTDIR\` is dropped. Other variables stay as folder names (`$PLUGINSDIR/nsExec.dll`,
  `$SYSDIR/…`, `$APPDATA/…`, `$0/…`). `SafeFs` makes drive letters, `..` and reserved names safe,
  as for every format.
- **More files:**
  - `WriteUninstaller` as a file;
  - Library.nsh's `InstallLib` temporary name (`$R0`) replaced by the library's;
  - a `FileOpen` and `FileClose` pair as an empty file.
- **Order:** by data position, with a name repeated at one position listed once.
- **Times and attributes:** the time is the instruction's (none for plugins); the attributes come
  from a `SetFileAttributes` right after it.
- **Differences from 7-Zip:** the uninstaller is listed when 7-Zip's instruction-layout guess
  leaves it out, and NSIS 2 ANSI names are read as UTF-8 first.
- **Sizes are exact** (decision 5). In a solid installer, the reader decodes the stream once at
  open and reads each record's length. In a non-solid one, a stored record's length is its size,
  and the compressed records are decoded at open.

### 4.2 Extraction

- **CramFS:** files in data order, each block decoded into one reused buffer; holes as zeros; one
  thread (116 MB in 0.28 s, §14).
- **NSIS, solid:** one pass over the stream, each record written to every file that names it.
- **NSIS, non-solid:** each record decoded on its own, in position order.
- **The uninstaller** is the stub, patched, then its data, as NSIS writes it. With no stub (a
  bare NSIS file), it's named `….nsis`, as 7-Zip names it.
- **Memory:**
  - CramFS: one block.
  - NSIS: the LZMA dictionary (8 MiB by default, 64 MiB with `/FINAL`; capped by
    `decoder_memory`), bzip2's 900 kB block (3.6 MB as BeeZip holds it), or deflate's 32 KiB
    window.

### 4.3 One entry

Preview, `entry.read` and `open_entry` read a CramFS file directly, and a non-solid NSIS record
directly. In a solid installer they decode from the start up to the record, as for a solid 7z.

### 4.4 Test and Info

- **Test** checks the CRC, when the image or installer has one, and decodes every file. A file that
  fails is `Corrupt`, named; a CRC mismatch is `Corrupt` for the whole file.
- **Info:**
  - CramFS: the name, edition, byte order, block size, the flags set (sorted folders, holes,
    extended pointers), the file count and whether the CRC was checked.
  - NSIS: NSIS 2 or 3, ANSI, Unicode or Park; installer or uninstaller; the method (with its
    dictionary or the x86 filter); solid or not; the header's size, the instruction count and
    the stub's size.

## 5. Limits and damage

### 5.1 Checked when it opens

- **CramFS:**
  - the superblock within the file, and only the flags Linux knows;
  - folders within the image, never entered twice, at most 256 deep (7-Zip's limit);
  - entries within their folder, and names of 1 to 252 bytes;
  - no more entries than `max_entries`;
  - block pointers that never go backwards and stay in the image;
  - no block over twice the block size (Linux's check), and each block decoding to exactly its
    size.
- **NSIS:**
  - first-header flags NSIS knows (detection already checks), and a header of at most 64 MiB
    (the largest seen is 868 KB);
  - instructions within the header, and string offsets within the table;
  - each string at most 64 KiB decoded, and each path at most 32 KiB;
  - no more files than `max_entries`, and the LZMA dictionary within `decoder_memory`;
  - each record within the file (non-solid) or the stream (solid);
  - the uninstaller's patches within the stub;
  - the ratio limit on each non-solid record against its compressed bytes, and on a solid
    stream against the installer's size.

### 5.2 Damage

`Corrupt`, and never handed to 7-Zip (decision 6):
- **A file cut after its header or directory** lists whole. The files before the cut extract, and
  the rest fail as cut short.
  - NSIS: BeeZip's own installer cut in half gives 6 of 12 files, a 21 MB solid one 250 of
    1,824 (7-Zip 248).
  - CramFS: the `/usr` image cut in half gives 330 of its 1,948 files and symlinks.
  - lzma-rust2 must defer its errors to get every byte before the cut.
  - A solid bzip2 installer cut inside its first 900 kB block gives nothing (7-Zip neither).
- **A CRC mismatch** fails `test`. Extraction goes on, as 7-Zip's does, and any file that doesn't
  decode fails.
- **An entry or record pointing past the data** is listed, and fails when it's read.

### 5.3 What goes to 7-Zip

`UnsupportedOperation`, for 7-Zip when it's installed:
- **CramFS:** flags Linux doesn't know, such as 7-Zip's LZMA variant.
- **NSIS:** an LZMA filter byte other than 0 or 1, and a header no NSIS 2, 3 or Park layout
  explains. None of the 55 installers has either.

## 13. Decisions

Approved by the user on 2026-11-12: every recommendation ("Yes"). Each is followed by its
alternative.

1. **CramFS: BeeZip's own reader,** with no crate (§1). *Alternative:* fstool 0.5.2's `cramfs`
   feature, which reads the same variants but adds `compcol`, a second inflate, to the build.
2. **The CramFS block size is read from the layout,** 4 to 64 KiB (§3.1). *Alternative:* 4 KiB
   only, as 7-Zip and x86 Linux read it; images for 16 or 64 KiB pages then fail.
3. **NSIS: BeeZip's own reader,** with its own inflater for NSIS 2's stored blocks and its own
   decoder for NSIS's bzip2 (§3.2). *Alternative:* `nsis` 0.6.0. It holds the installer and its
